Team Artificially Unintelligent

For a submission to the 6th Hack-Nation Global AI Hackathon, we built this application. A clinical-support prototype for women's hormonal health that combines multiple specialised models instead of trusting a single predictor — then connects the result to a shared patient record workflow, role-based access, and a safety-aware health chatbot.

The idea

Don't throw away imperfect models. Make their limitations useful.

The project challenges the idea that one dataset or one model should carry the full decision. Instead, different sources of evidence contribute to a combined output, while the application keeps the final interpretation in the hands of a clinician.

01

Dataset bias

Generic clinical models may inherit mixed-sex or male-skewed training distributions that do not represent women's hormonal health equally well.

02

Narrow specialisation

Women-only models can be more targeted, but a narrow cohort may not capture every relevant signal or comorbidity on its own.

03

Fragmented records

Patient history is often distributed across separate providers. The prototype explores a shared record layer with controlled patient and doctor permissions.

System architecture

Multiple signals in. One clinically focused result out.

Uploaded lab and screening information is transformed into structured features and evaluated through complementary model paths. Their outputs are combined into a final result rather than treating any individual model as absolute ground truth.

Thyroid cohort

Model B

PCOS cohort

Model C

Clinical criteria

Application layer

AI is only one part of the product.

The prototype wraps the model inside a role-aware healthcare workflow, separating what patients can do from what clinicians can do and providing a human escalation path for chatbot interactions.

◫

Shared health records

Patient history from participating providers is presented through a common record view, reducing the need to navigate isolated hospital portals.

⌁

Role-based permissions

Patients can read and download their records, while clinicians additionally receive upload access for adding new reports and screening results.

✦

Safety-aware chatbot

The health assistant is positioned as an informational interface rather than a diagnostic tool. Serious queries can be summarised and escalated for doctor review.

∿

Human-in-the-loop analysis

The model surfaces evidence for further investigation; the clinician remains responsible for interpreting the result and making the medical decision.

Datasets

Two cohorts with deliberately different perspectives.

The project uses a women-only PCOS cohort alongside a mixed-sex thyroid cohort, allowing the system to explore how specialised and generic data sources can contribute differently.

PCOS Cohort

Prasoon Kottarathil · Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) Dataset

541 women Hormone panels Ultrasound Clinical symptoms
  • Clinician-confirmed PCOS labels
  • Menstrual-cycle history
  • Follicle and ultrasound measurements

Thyroid Cohort

UCI Machine Learning Repository · Thyroid Disease / Hypothyroid Dataset

~3,160 patients 69% female 31% male Thyroid labs
  • TSH, T3, T4, T4U and FTI
  • Age and sex features
  • Pregnancy and acute-illness indicators
Prototype context: this project was created as an AI hackathon prototype and is not a medical diagnostic device. Its design intentionally keeps diagnosis and clinical judgment with healthcare professionals.
Implementation

Built as an end-to-end ML + web application prototype.

The documented implementation centres on a Flask application, reproducible dataset processing, machine-learning inference, persistent patient records, and an AI-assisted patient interaction layer.
